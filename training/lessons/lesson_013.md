# PSA first-party grading standards

**This is the only first-party source in the corpus.** Every other lesson records what a
practitioner believes PSA does. This records what PSA says it does, from PSA's own video
and PSA's own published Grade Definitions page.

## THE PUBLISHED DEFINITION, VERBATIM

Under **Grade Definitions - GEM-MT - PSA 10**:

> A PSA Gem Mint 10 card is a virtually perfect card.
>
> Attributes include four perfectly sharp corners, sharp focus and full original gloss.
> A PSA Gem Mint 10 card must be free of staining of any kind, but an allowance may be
> made for a slight printing imperfection, if it doesn't impair the overall appeal of the
> card. The image must be centered on the card within a tolerance not to exceed
> approximately 55/45 percent on the front, and 75/25 percent on the reverse.

## WHAT THE VIDEO ADDS

PSA states its graders "primarily focus on four things" and defines each as a question:
centering within the borders; corners sharp versus frame bending or wear; visible surface
imperfections "that might be difficult to see with the naked eye"; edges smooth and crisp
versus chipping or tears. [00:26-00:47]

It then demonstrates the 10-versus-9 boundary twice: one pair differing only in centering,
and one pair where "closer inspection reveals a small white touch on one corner. A minor
defect like this can be enough to bring down the grade." [01:00-01:20]

And: "A grade may be lowered merely due to a technical defect, like a minor crease or paper
loss on the back" even where eye appeal is strong. [01:30-01:40]

## VISUAL EXAMPLES
PSA's own macro photography of a slabbed card corner at the white-touch demonstration

## WHAT THIS CONFIRMS
- The 55/45 front and 75/25 back figures the owner supplied are verbatim correct.
- CORNERS_TOUCH_001 - a corner touch costing a 10 - is now stated by PSA itself.
- IMAGE_LIMITATIONS_SURFACE_001 - surface being hard to see - is stated by PSA itself
  ("difficult to see with the naked eye"). Three instructors and now the grader agree.
- PROCESS_CHECKLIST_001's four categories are PSA's own four.

## WHAT THIS CONTRADICTS - REQUIRES ATTENTION
**SURFACE_PRINT_LINE_ACROSS_001** is active and states a print line across the face "is
penalised every time and prevents a high grade". PSA's published standard explicitly allows
"a slight printing imperfection, if it doesn't impair the overall appeal of the card".

These are not flatly incompatible - the MLP instructor was describing a prominent line
across a player's face, which plausibly does impair overall appeal - but "every time" is
wrong as written. The rule overstates a practitioner's observation into an absolute that
the published standard contradicts. It is superseded below.

## A NOTE ON "APPROXIMATELY"
The standard says "a tolerance not to exceed **approximately** 55/45". That word is doing
real work, and it reconciles CENTERING_PRODUCT_LENIENCY_001 - which observes PSA granting
leeway on poorly-cut products - with the published figure. The threshold is not a hard
arithmetic cutoff, and a reviewer should not treat 56/44 as an automatic failure.
